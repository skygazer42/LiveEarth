## Design Context

### Users

LiveEarth serves globally curious viewers who want to open a page and immediately witness something remarkable happening somewhere on Earth. The primary use is an immersive, AI-directed global channel; ranking and globe controls support exploration without turning the experience into a weather tool or operations dashboard. Desktop is the cinematic reference experience, while mobile retains the full viewing, ranking, globe, favorites, and history flow.

### Brand Personality

Editorial, cinematic, alive. LiveEarth should feel like a quietly confident documentary newsroom that is always watching the planet. Copy is concise and observant, never sensational or falsely certain. The interface should create awe, presence, and curiosity while leaving the live image in control.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a filmic, full-bleed visual system inspired by documentary title cards and broadcast program guides: ink-tinted blacks, warm paper whites, mineral blue, and one restrained signal red for live state. Typography pairs an expressive high-contrast editorial serif with a humane grotesk. Layouts are asymmetric and image-led, with deliberate rules, timestamps, location type, and restrained motion. Avoid neon sci-fi dashboards, cyan-purple gradients, glassmorphism, generic floating cards, dense weather telemetry, and rounded SaaS UI.

The experience launches in English and Simplified Chinese. It is dark-first because live footage is the canvas, not because the product is a glowing technology interface. Original environment audio is available only after user action; autoplay begins muted.

### Design Principles

1. The live scene is always the largest and highest-contrast element; controls recede until needed.
2. Explain editorial judgment in one precise sentence and expose evidence without pretending AI certainty.
3. Use the globe as spatial storytelling: fly with intention, then become still enough to watch.
4. Make ranking changes legible and stable; the product should feel directed, not algorithmically frantic.
5. Preserve every core task on mobile and for keyboard/reduced-motion users, adapting composition instead of removing capability.
